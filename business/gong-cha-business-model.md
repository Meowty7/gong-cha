# Gong Cha — Business Model Analysis

## Model at a Glance

Gong Cha operates a **B2B franchise-led expansion model** with a B2C consumer brand on top. The corporate entity does not primarily sell tea — it sells the *right to operate a Gong Cha store* and the *system* behind it. Revenue flows from franchise fees, royalties, marketing fees, and supply chain, while franchisees capture in-store consumer revenue.

## Core Revenue Streams (corporate / franchisor)

1. **Initial franchise / development fees** — paid on signing and per-store opening.
2. **Ongoing royalties** — described as "highly competitive" (specific % not disclosed publicly).
3. **Marketing fees** — contribution to regional/global marketing fund.
4. **Supply chain margin** — ingredients and proprietary inputs sourced through Gong Cha's quality-control lab and supply network.
5. **Master franchise agreements** — territory-level deals (e.g. the 300-store Middle East master agreement with Shahia Foods Group) — the highest-leverage form of expansion.

## Customer Segments

| Segment | Who | What they buy |
|---|---|---|
| End consumer | Bubble tea drinkers, students, young professionals | A premium, customizable beverage experience |
| Single-unit franchisee | Local entrepreneurs | One store, full operational system |
| Multi-unit developer | Regional operators | Territory development rights (several stores) |
| Master franchisee | Large F&B operators (e.g. Shahia Foods) | Country/region rights + sub-franchising |

## Value Proposition to Franchisees

Stated on the franchise page, Gong Cha sells **eight pillars**:

1. **Simple to Run** — labour-efficient, streamlined ops, smart training.
2. **Scalable Model** — easy to add stores and manage territory.
3. **Quality Control** — in-house QC lab ensures product consistency.
4. **Smart Formats** — stores need no grease traps or cooking exhaust, so they fit almost anywhere (kiosks, in-line, flagship).
5. **Appealing Store Design** — "sleek, clean" premium look vs. mom-and-pop competitors; mix of traditional accents + modern touches.
6. **Flexible Footprints** — from 100 sq ft kiosks to 2,500 sq ft flagships; downtown corners, strip malls, university campuses, shop-in-shop.
7. **Operational Processes** — standardized beverage prep, POS, order kiosks, in-store marketing.
8. **International Leadership & Support** — global + regional support centres, online training videos, manuals, marketing assets.

Additional moats: **Smart Inventory Management** (ingredients don't require large refrigeration → lower capex), **award-winning QSR brand recognition**, and a **loyal customer base**.

## Unit Economics (publicly disclosed)

| Item | Range |
|---|---|
| Kiosk (from 100 sq ft) | from **$100,000** initial investment |
| Flagship store (up to 2,500 sq ft) | from **$350,000+** |
| Time to open (existing market) | a few weeks – 3 months |
| Time to open (new market) | 4 – 8 months |

Royalty and marketing fee percentages are not disclosed on the public site ("highly competitive"; disclosed via FDD where required).

## Store Format Strategy

- **Kiosk** — small footprint, low capex, high-traffic locations (malls, transit).
- **In-line / shop-in-shop** — flexible, can be dual-branded (separate POS, physical partition preferred).
- **Flagship** — premium experience, larger footprint, brand-building.
- **Dual-branded** — explicitly supported; each brand runs independently.

This format flexibility is a core competitive advantage: it lets Gong Cha penetrate dense urban cores (kiosks) and suburban/destination retail (flagships) with the same brand.

## Ideal Franchisee Profile (stated)

1. **Growth mindset** — drive to expand global footprint.
2. **Leadership skills** — confidence to lead in the bubble tea market.
3. **Commitment to service** — passion for exceptional customer experience.
4. **Portfolio synergy** — a scalable addition to an existing brand portfolio.

The fourth point reveals Gong Cha's preferred partner: **existing multi-brand F&B operators**, not first-time entrepreneurs. This matches the master-franchise deals (Shahia Foods).

## Growth Strategy

- **Geographic** — 33 markets today, targeting new territories (Middle East, Reunion Island, etc.).
- **Density** — multi-unit development agreements in existing markets.
- **Brand collaborations** — e.g. PEANUTS limited-edition tumblers in the Americas → cultural/IP collabs as marketing.
- **Value promotions** — UK "Tea-riple Tea-hreat" deal addresses cost-of-living pressure without diluting premium positioning.

## Competitive Positioning

Gong Cha sits in the **premium-but-accessible** tier of the bubble tea market:
- Above: independent artisanal tea shops (better ops, brand, scale).
- Below: commodity powder-based boba chains (better ingredients, QC, store design).
- Peers: CoCo, Kung Fu Tea, Sharetea, Heytea — differentiated by whole-leaf brewing, QC lab, and global franchise system.

## Key Risks / Watch-items

- Franchise-heavy models depend on franchisee unit economics; royalty % not public.
- Bubble tea is a trendy category — menu innovation cadence (Famous Five + seasonal LTOs) is critical.
- Expansion into new markets (Middle East, Reunion) requires localization (flavor profiles, halal considerations, supply chain).
- Master franchise concentration: a few large territory deals drive a disproportionate share of growth.
